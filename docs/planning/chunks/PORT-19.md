# PORT-19 — Reuse the factorisation across the drives of one sweep

 **Reuse the factorisation across the drives of one sweep** — one factorisation per sweep instead of one per port. *Opened 2026-09-10, operator direction, on measured cost.* **The premise, verified in code before this was scoped:** in `run_lumped_sheet_port_case` the `extra_bilinear_terms` list is built over **every** port id and references only `sheet.sheet_resistivity` and `ω` (`ports/lumped.py:484–489`), while `extra_linear_terms` references **only** `sheets[driven_port_id]` (`:490–493`). The system matrix is therefore the same for every drive of a matched-termination sweep and only the right-hand side changes — the textbook multiple-RHS case. Today each drive builds its own `LinearProblem` and re-factorises: the `ANS-4` step 2d log shows one `factorize+solve starting` per drive, fifteen for fifteen drives. **The cost, measured on that run** (`-n 16`, degree 2, 128 MHz): 743 k unknowns 42 s/drive, 1.80 M unknowns 280 s/drive, 3.83 M unknowns **1335 s/drive** — and the factorisation is almost all of it. Consequence for the deliverable: a 32-port human-scale sweep at the resolution `ANS-4` matched AED at is ≈ 2.5 M unknowns × 32 drives ≈ **5.4 h**, which fits no window; with reuse it is one factorisation plus 32 back-substitutions, **≈ 20 min**. That is worth more than the 512 → 754 GiB tier and more than extra cores, and it is why `xxl` is a safety net rather than the fix. **Step 1 — prove the premise by measurement, not by reading** (smoke, `-n 2`, no new solver path): assemble the sweep's matrix for drive 1 and for drive 2 on the gated 4-leg fixture and assert they are **bit-identical** — same nonzero pattern, and `‖A₁ − A₂‖_∞ == 0.0` exactly, not to a tolerance — while the two right-hand sides differ (`‖b₁ − b₂‖₂ > 0`; *the earlier wording "by more than the reference impedance" compared a vector norm to ohms and was struck 2026-09-11 03:00 review*). If they are *not* identical the chunk is void and the finding is the negative result: say which term carries the drive and stop. **Step 2 — implement** (standard, `-n 2`): factor once per sweep and solve N right-hand sides, in `ports/sparameters.py`, behind a default-on keyword so the per-drive path stays reachable for a control. `dolfinx`'s `LinearProblem` discards its factor with the object, so the reuse has to hold the `PETSc` `KSP`/`PC` across solves — `core/time_harmonic.py:563–579` already documents that holding it holds the MUMPS factor and names the peak-memory tradeoff, which here is *cheaper* than today because one factor is held instead of N built in sequence. **Gates, every band imported and unmoved:** the 4-leg 4×4 at 10 MHz reproduces `PORT-9` leg (d)'s recorded `S` to **1e-12 or better** (it is the same arithmetic, so anything looser is a defect, not a tolerance); reciprocity, σ_max and the C4 class spreads unchanged; and the per-drive route re-run in the same window agrees with the reused route entry by entry. **Negative control, asserted:** with reuse forced on a sweep whose drives *do* change the matrix (perturb one port's `port_impedance_ohm`), the reused result must differ from the per-drive result by ≥ 100× the reproduction band — i.e. the optimisation is provably unsafe exactly where its premise fails, so a future change that breaks the premise cannot pass silently. **Printed, gated by nothing:** wall time per drive before and after, and the factor's memory. **Done-when:** both steps ✅, the 32-port `PORT-13` sweep re-run and its recorded 32×32 reproduced, the speedup recorded as a measurement rather than a claim. **Not in scope:** iterative solvers, preconditioners, and any change to the port model — this chunk changes when a factorisation happens and nothing else. **Step 1 recorded 2026-09-10 21:00 slot — premise holds, measured:** on the gated 4-leg fixture (116 085 cells, 139 140 N1curl(1) dofs, 10 MHz, `-n 2`, complex) the sum of the four `lumped_port_bilinear_term`s assembled for drives P1…P4 has one nonzero pattern and `‖A_k − A_1‖_∞ = 0.0` exactly for every k, with 0 differing CSR values (`20260911T020253Z_PORT-19-step1.log:1832–1835`);
